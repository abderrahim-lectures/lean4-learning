\chapter{Chapter 5: Rigor check --- structures, universes, and equality}\label{sec:05-rigor-check:00-index:1}

\begin{learningobjectives}
\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Explain why this book delays \texttt{class} in favor of \texttt{structure}.
\item
  State the STLC typing rules and why \texttt{Type} itself needs a universe hierarchy.
\item
  Distinguish definitional from propositional equality, and predict when \texttt{rfl} alone will and will not close a goal.
\end{itemize}
\end{learningobjectives}


Before committing to the definition of \texttt{Group} in the next chapter, this short chapter answers three questions a careful mathematician should already be asking. Why does this book use plain \texttt{structure} instead of the \texttt{class} mechanism in Lean, which is what Mathlib actually uses? What exactly is \texttt{Type}, and does \texttt{Group (α : Type)} really quantify over \emph{all} types, including \texttt{Group} itself? And when two proofs both establish \texttt{a = b}, in what sense are they ``the same''? Skipping these would leave exactly the kind of unstated assumption a demanding reader is trained to notice and question, so they are addressed here, before Chapter 6 proceeds.

Unlike Chapters 1--3, this chapter is not building new machinery toward \texttt{Group}. It is pausing to justify choices about to be made silently. Each section answers one of the three questions raised above, in the order a skeptical reader would actually ask them.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Mathlib defines every algebraic structure with \texttt{class}, not \texttt{structure}. If this book is heading toward the same kind of definitions, why not just use the tool the professionals use?} (\hyperref[sec:05-rigor-check:01-structure-vs-class:1]{Section 1}) Because \texttt{class} adds exactly one mechanism, automatic instance search, on top of plain \texttt{structure}, and that mechanism is worth understanding on purpose before leaning on it silently. This book delays it until the intuition for \texttt{Group} in Chapter 6 is already solid.
\item
  \textbf{A \texttt{structure} like \texttt{Group (G : Type)} is itself a term of some type. So does \texttt{Group} have a \texttt{Group}-structure of its own? Is \texttt{Group Group} a sensible thing to even ask for?} (\hyperref[sec:05-rigor-check:02-universes:1]{Section 2}) No, and the reason is load-bearing, not pedantic. \texttt{Type} cannot contain itself without reproducing Russell's paradox, so Lean stacks an infinite hierarchy of universes, and \texttt{Group : Type → Type} sits one level too high to ever be its own carrier.
\item
  \textbf{That answer leaned on a typing rule, ``a Π-type built from level \(i\) and \(j\) lands in level \(\max(i,j)\),'' without ever stating where such rules come from. What \emph{are} the typing rules of Lean, precisely, and why should they be trusted?} (\hyperref[sec:05-rigor-check:03-typing-rules-and-safety:1]{Section 3}) The three rules of the simply typed λ-calculus (Var, Abs, App), plus the universe-formation rule from Section 2 restated formally, together with the progress and preservation theorems that are the actual reason ``well-typed proofs do not go wrong.''
\item
  \textbf{Preservation guarantees reduction never changes the type of a term, but it says nothing about when two terms \emph{are} the same term. \texttt{rfl} has been used constantly since Chapter 3 without ever asking what ``the same'' means.} (\hyperref[sec:05-rigor-check:04-defeq-vs-propeq:1]{Section 4}) Two notions, not one: definitional equality (\texttt{rfl}, checked by computation) and propositional equality (\texttt{=}, proved like any other theorem) coincide often enough to blur together, but knowing exactly where they part ways is what makes a failed \texttt{rfl} legible instead of mysterious.
\end{enumerate}

By the end of the chapter, three assumptions Chapter 6 is about to make silently, \texttt{structure} over \texttt{class}, the place of \texttt{Group : Type → Type} in the universe hierarchy, and the exact reach of \texttt{rfl}, have each been earned rather than merely used.
