## v1.5.4 — Binder styles, `def` transparency, and arrow-notation coverage

A reader working through Chapter 1 flagged `def`/`let`/implicit arguments
as taught by omission. Only two of Lean's four binder styles were named
(explicit `()`, implicit `{}`), `def` was never contrasted with `abbrev`/
`opaque`, and the `→` symbol was never flagged as later meaning two more
things (implication, then the general Π-type) without comment. A second
gap, found while illustrating the first: the equation-style `def` body
(`def f : A → B | pat => ...`), used from Chapter 1 onward for every
`Vec` example, was never named as a distinct writing style, so its `→`
looked like a third unrelated arrow meaning rather than "argument matched
on, not bound by name."

This release adds the missing coverage at the point in the book where its
prerequisites already exist, rather than in Chapter 1 itself, matching the
book's own staged-teaching structure. Chapter 1 gets only short, honest
forward-pointers; the full bullet-by-bullet-plus-"Mathematical reading"
treatment lands in Chapter 4 (reducibility, once `unfold` is available)
and Chapter 5 (instance-implicit `[...]`, once `class`/`instance` exist).

### Additions

- **Chapter 1, Section 2** (`01-basics/02-def-let-implicit.md`) — a new
  closing subsection naming instance-implicit `[...]` and strict-implicit
  `⦃⦄` binders, and `abbrev`/`opaque` alongside `def`, each with a pointer
  to where it is explained in full. A short aside after the `double`
  example flags that `→` will later mean implication (Chapter 3) and is a
  special case of the general Π-type (this chapter's Section 5).
- **Chapter 1, Section 3** (`01-basics/03-dependent-types.md`) — a full
  side-by-side treatment of equation-style vs. named-binder `def`s, right
  where `Vec.replicate` first uses the equation-style form, with a minimal
  `vecLen`/`vecLen'` pair and a "Mathematical reading" box tying it to
  ordinary definition-by-cases notation.
- **Chapter 3, Section 4** (`03-propositions-and-proofs/04-implication.md`)
  — a backward callback naming `P → Q` as the same `→` from `Nat → Nat`
  in Chapter 1, now over `Prop`.
- **Chapter 3, Section 6** (`03-propositions-and-proofs/06-quantifiers.md`)
  — a one-line pointer at the existing `@[reducible] def isPrime` to the
  fuller Chapter 4 treatment.
- **Chapter 4** (`04-tactics/04-more-tactics.md`) — a new subsection on
  `def` (semi-reducible) vs. `abbrev` (reducible) vs. `opaque`
  transparency, next to `unfold`, with a "Mathematical reading" box. The
  demonstration deliberately uses only `rfl`/`unfold`, never `simp`
  (this chapter's own stated policy), and defers the "watch it matter in
  a proof" payoff to Chapter 5's typeclass search, since neither `rfl`
  behavior nor `simp` could honestly demonstrate the distinction with only
  Chapter 4's tools without an uncompiled, unverified claim about
  elaboration-transparency internals.
- **Chapter 5, Section 1** (`05-rigor-check/01-structure-vs-class.md`) —
  names `[...]` explicitly as the fourth binder style where `instance` is
  first introduced, contrasting "solved by typeclass search" against `{}`'s
  "solved by unification."
- **`tactic-and-library-reference.md`** — a new "Binder & definition
  styles" lookup table, in the same two-column shape as the existing
  `## Tactics` table, indexing all of the above.

### Notes

- No claim is made anywhere about `rfl` succeeding or failing on a plain
  `def` without `unfold` first. No Lean toolchain was available in this
  environment to compile-verify that specific behavior, and the book's own
  standard (see v1.5.1's note on the uncompiled `Vec` snippet) is to write
  prose that claims only what is independently verifiable, not to assert
  an interactive trace that cannot be checked. **Re-run `lake build` in
  `lean_project/` before treating the new `Vec` snippet in Chapter 1 §3 as
  compile-verified.**
- `changelog/README.md`'s index was missing its `v1.5.3` line; added
  alongside this entry.
