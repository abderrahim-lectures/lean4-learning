\section{What forces tactic mode}\label{sec:05-tactics:00-index:2}

Writing proof \emph{terms} directly (as in Chapter 4) quickly becomes hard to manage as a proof grows. The alternative is \textbf{tactic mode}, entered with \texttt{by}, in which a ``goal'' is worked step by step, much as one would write a proof on paper. The real subject of this chapter is not the list of tactics below, but \textbf{how to work a goal whose proof is not yet known}, since that is the skill the rest of the book practices. The tactic reference is secondary, read it once, then return to it as needed. When a later exercise leaves you unsure which tactic to try, the proof strategy guide maps the \emph{shape} of a goal or hypothesis directly to a first move for just that case.

The anatomy of a goal, and the habit of reading every hypothesis before trying anything, comes first (\hyperref[sec:05-tactics:01-goal-state:1]{Section 1}). Four tactics carry the bulk of any proof, \texttt{intro}, \texttt{exact}, \texttt{apply}, \texttt{rw}, each mapped to the ordinary inference rule it realizes (\hyperref[sec:05-tactics:02-core-tactics:1]{Section 2}). When a tactic fails, Lean does not whisper, it points; reading an error message as a debugging trace, and telling apart a \texttt{sorry} that is a placeholder to be filled from one flagging that something deeper must change, comes next (\hyperref[sec:05-tactics:03-reading-failures:1]{Section 3}). Reasoning by cases or by induction on data that is defined recursively needs its own tactics, \texttt{cases}, \texttt{induction}, \texttt{constructor}, \texttt{unfold}, the tools that open up inductively-defined data (\hyperref[sec:05-tactics:04-more-tactics:1]{Section 4}). Assembling all of the above into a real inductive proof from scratch, with no automation to hide behind, commutativity of \texttt{Nat.add}, built one tactic at a time, closes the loop (\hyperref[sec:05-tactics:05-worked-example:1]{Section 5}).
