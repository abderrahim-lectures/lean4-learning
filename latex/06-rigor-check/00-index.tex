\chapter{Chapter 6: Rigor check --- structures, universes, and equality}\label{sec:06-rigor-check:00-index:1}

\begin{learningobjectives}
\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Explain why this book delays \texttt{class} in favor of \texttt{structure}.
\item
  State the STLC typing rules and why \texttt{Type} itself needs a universe hierarchy.
\item
  Distinguish definitional from propositional equality, and predict when \texttt{rfl} alone will and will not close a goal.
\end{itemize}
\end{learningobjectives}


Before committing to the definition of \texttt{Group} in the next chapter, three choices about to be made silently need to be earned first. Mathlib defines every algebraic structure with \texttt{class}, not plain \texttt{structure}; Section 1 asks what \texttt{class} actually buys beyond \texttt{structure}, finds the answer is exactly one mechanism (automatic instance search), and gives the reason this book delays leaning on it. A \texttt{structure} like \texttt{Group (G : Type)} is itself a term of some type, so Section 2 asks whether \texttt{Group} has a \texttt{Group}-structure of its own, and the answer, no, forces the universe hierarchy \texttt{Type : Type 1 : Type 2 : ...} into view, since \texttt{Type : Type} would reproduce the Russell paradox. That argument leaned on an unstated typing rule, so Section 3 states the typing rules of Lean precisely, the three rules of the simply typed λ-calculus (Var, Abs, App) plus the universe-formation rule of Section 2, together with the progress and preservation theorems that are the actual reason well-typed proofs do not go wrong. Preservation guarantees reduction never changes the type of a term, but says nothing about when two terms \emph{are} the same term, and \texttt{rfl} has been used constantly since Chapter 4 without that question ever being asked; Section 4 separates definitional equality (\texttt{rfl}, checked by computation) from propositional equality (\texttt{=}, proved like any theorem), close enough to blur together but diverging exactly where a failed \texttt{rfl} needs to become legible rather than mysterious.

By the end of the chapter, three assumptions Chapter 7 is about to make silently, \texttt{structure} over \texttt{class}, the place of \texttt{Group : Type → Type} in the universe hierarchy, and the exact reach of \texttt{rfl}, have each been earned rather than merely used.
